# Decision Log Entry - HOK-2815

Date: 2026-09-24 America/New_York

Entry:

**2026-09-24 · HOK-2815 · wavemill → data pipeline / website / SDK** — Arbiter R6 lands as an analysis-only reconnaissance tool that measures net pair yield before and after the reviewer-stage fork. The report is a pure, reproducible aggregation over the deduplicated `challenge-records.jsonl` and `evals.jsonl` corpus: the unit of analysis is one `challengePairId` (honouring voids and supersession), not an eval row. Cohorts are classified from persisted fork descriptors (pre-fork: `sharedPrefix !== true` or no reviewer fork identity; reviewer-fork post: `sharedPrefix === true` AND `forkStage === 'review'`), never from a date heuristic; other fork stages and incomplete provenance are reported separately in the exclusions bucket. Delivery yield (pairs producing a comparison) and valid stage-label yield (successful-fork pairs with `stageAttribution.status === 'valid'`) are reported separately per §21 and the September 9 valid-label update. The seven-step native launch funnel from the September 22 rollout gate is reconstructed from durable intent, route/execution attestations, deferred-arm/fork evidence, terminal records, and validity fields; transitions lacking durable evidence are marked `unobservable` rather than guessed, and `native`/`native-openrouter` are normalized through the shared selection-health canonical identity helper. Cost and elapsed time are reported per delivery-usable pair and per valid reviewer label only when their denominators are non-zero, with explicit unavailable counts. Counterfactual accounting separates historical forfeits from pre-fork primary failures that would have been usable forfeits under the old scheme. Recommendation logic is conservative and explainable: coder-stage fork is `Go` only when reviewer-fork delivery yield and cost/time per usable pair are not worse than the pre-fork baseline, all September 22 rollout gates pass, `challenge.rate` was unchanged during the measurement window, coding-ready native supply is positive, and at least two native identities have fresh passing coding canaries. Otherwise the tool renders `No-Go` or `No-Go: insufficient evidence` and leaves coder arms independent. Valid-label yield remains evidence-quality telemetry only; after the HOK-2073 B1 No-Go it does not authorize reviewer-router training. Local worktrees have no `.wavemill/evals` corpus, so this landing does not itself make the empirical call — the tool preserves an explicit insufficient-evidence default until a supplied production corpus is analyzed. This is analysis only: no database migration, runtime routing change, or `challenge.rate` change.
